\section{Related Work}
Beyond developing individual foundation models, recent studies have increasingly focused on benchmarking and comparing the representation capability of different pathology FMs.
These efforts typically evaluate foundation models based on downstream predictive performance, transferability across datasets, and robustness under different clinical tasks \citep{lei2026validation, boutaj2026from, vanderbilt2026goldmark}.

With the emergence of multiple pathology FMs, recent works have explored strategies for integrating heterogeneous representations to improve downstream performance.
A straightforward approach is representation concatenation or averaging, where features from multiple encoders are aggregated before prediction. 
These approaches implicitly assume that different encoders provide complementary information or that downstream classifiers can effectively extract task-relevant signals from the aggregated representations.

More recent methods introduce learnable fusion mechanisms, including gated fusion and attention-based aggregation, to adaptively adjust the contribution of different representations.
For example, Meta-Encoder proposed a unified framework for integrating multiple pathology foundation models and systematically evaluated different fusion strategies across cancer detection tasks \citep{gao2026metaencoder}.
Other studies explored adaptive integration through logit-level fusion \citep{huang2026plugandplay}, prompt-guided fusion \citep{xiao2025adafusion}, group-based encoder selection \citep{quan2025gas}, and ensemble learning frameworks for precision oncology \citep{luo2025elf}.
Recent works such as Shazam and FuseCPath further investigated unified integration of multiple foundation models under heterogeneous pathology settings \citep{lei2026shazam, yang2025fusecpath}.

Recent studies also have started exploring MoE-style architectures in computational pathology. Liu et al. proposed a multimodal mixture-of-experts framework that integrates pathology foundation models for immune biomarker prediction, demonstrating the potential of expert specialization for precision oncology \citep{liu2026predicting}.
Tian et al. introduced region-graph optimal transport routing for MoE-based whole-slide image classification, leveraging structured routing strategies to select informative experts or regions during prediction\citep{tian2026regiongraph}.

Meanwhile, understanding relationships among learned representations has been widely studied in machine learning.
Representation similarity analysis methods, such as centered kernel alignment (CKA)\citep{cka}, have been used to quantify similarities between neural networks and characterize whether different models learn similar feature spaces.
Recent studies have extended such analyses to computational pathology foundation models, revealing heterogeneous representation structures across different encoders \citep{mishra2025comparing}.
However, existing representation analyses mainly characterize similarity patterns, while how representation composition influences multi-foundation-model fusion behavior remains largely unexplored.
