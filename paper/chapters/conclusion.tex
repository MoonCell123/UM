\section{Conclusion}
In this work, we demonstrate that multi-foundation-model fusion performance is not simply improved by increasing the number of integrated models. 
Fusion effectiveness varies with representation composition, while representation similarity alone is insufficient to characterize functional redundancy or task-level utility. 
These findings motivate the design of CoR-Fuse: consensus conditioning provides cross-encoder context for handling heterogeneous representation compositions, while sample-specific routing accounts for variation in encoder contributions across slides.
Our study has several limitations.
First, experiments are conducted on relatively small TCGA cohorts, particularly TCGA-UVM, where performance differences should be interpreted in light of cross-validation variance. 
Second, although we evaluate CoR-Fuse on two TCGA cohorts, these datasets do not constitute fully independent external validation across institutions or acquisition settings. 
Future studies will assess the method on larger, multi-institutional cohorts and under domain-shift scenarios.
Third, our evaluation focuses on diagnostic and molecular classification tasks, and future work will extend CoR-Fuse to prognosis-oriented endpoints such as survival prediction.
