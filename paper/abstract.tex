\begin{abstract}
Recent pathology foundation models have achieved remarkable performance across
diverse pathology tasks. However, different foundation models capture distinct 
morphological and semantic patterns, while a single model may fail to fully 
characterize complex pathological features. Fusing heterogeneous
representations from multiple foundation models can leverage their complementary
information and achieve more comprehensive pathological representation.
Existing fusion strategies mainly rely on feature concatenation
or uniform aggregation, implicitly assuming that all foundation models contribute
equally and contain complementary information. However, representations from 
different foundation models may exhibit substantial redundancy, and their individual
contributions remain unclear. Although attention-based fusion methods enable adaptive
weighting, the learned weights lack explicit interpretability regarding the
importance of each foundation model. To address these limitations, we propose

intervention-based strategy, providing an explicit measurement of model importance.
