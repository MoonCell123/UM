\begin{abstract}
The emergence of pathology foundation models (FMs) provides diverse visual representations for computational pathology, yet how to effectively integrate multiple FMs remains unclear. 
Existing fusion approaches typically aggregate multiple foundation models through fixed or implicitly learned fusion mechanisms, without explicitly considering how encoder contributions vary with representation composition and individual samples.
To address this gap, we conduct a three-level analysis of multi-foundation-model fusion, spanning representation-level geometry, feature-level rank dependence, and task-level pairwise interaction.
Through this analysis, we show that fusion effectiveness is influenced by representation composition rather than the number of integrated models alone. 
We further reveal that representation similarity does not necessarily indicate task-level redundancy, and encoder contributions vary substantially across individual slides. 
These observations suggest that effective fusion requires adaptive utilization of foundation models based on sample-specific representation characteristics. 
Motivated by these findings, we propose CoR-Fuse, a consensus-aware two-stage routing fusion framework for adaptive multi-foundation-model integration. 
Experimental results on TCGA-UVM and TCGA-BLCA demonstrate that CoR-Fuse maintains competitive and stable predictive performance across the evaluated foundation-model compositions, while substantially reducing the parameter count and inference FLOPs relative to attention-based fusion strategies.
Code is released in the supplementary materials.
\end{abstract}
