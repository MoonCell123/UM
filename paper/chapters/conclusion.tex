\section{Conclusion}
In this work, we reveal that multi-foundation-model fusion not be viewed as a simple model-scaling problem. 
The usefulness of an encoder depends on the representation composition in which it is used, while similarity between representations alone does not reliably indicate redundancy or task-level utility. 
Our analyses also show that the relative contribution of foundation models can vary substantially across slides, suggesting that a single universal fusion strategy is insufficient for heterogeneous pathology representations. 
We propose CoR-Fuse, a consensus-aware routing fusion framework that estimates sample-specific encoder weights from encoder-specific slide descriptors and cross-encoder consensus representations.
Overall understanding representation composition and adapting encoder contributions are more important for scalable foundation-model integration than simply increasing the number of participating models.
Future work will evaluate CoR-Fuse on broader multi-center cohorts and domain-shift scenarios, and explore adaptive routing under dynamic foundation-model availability.
